When a lone drifter with partial memory loss, Isaac, accepts a job to look after his landlord's niece, Olga, for a few days it all seems like easy money, but there is a caveat: he must wear a leather harness and chain that restricts his movements to certain rooms. Once Olga's uncle, Barrett, leaves the two of them alone, a game of cat and mouse ensues as Olga displays continuingly worse psychological issues. With Isaac trapped and at the mercy of Olga, he begins to discover secrets in the house that coincide with the terrifying resurgence of his own memory.

An effective spine-tingler despite clear budget constraints, Caveat suggests a deliciously dark filmmaking future for writer-director Damian Mc Carthy.

See more Marco L @decatur555 Aug 18, 2026 Caveat has a very good setup and several genuinely terrifying moments, even if its protagonist accepts conditions that no sensible person would ever agree to. Once you get past that, Damian Mc Carthy creates a wonderfully oppressive atmosphere with very little: a decaying house, an isolated island, dark corridors, strange noises and one deeply unsettling rabbit with a drum. The film works best when it relies on discomfort rather than jump scares, making you feel that something may be hiding behind every wall or doorway. The pacing is uneven and the mystery becomes more complicated than necessary, so I would not recommend it to everyone. But if you want ninety minutes of claustrophobic tension and a few moments of maximum dread, Caveat definitely delivers. See more Brant F @destroyfascism Jun 19, 2026 A remarkably effective low-budget horror film that creates a constant sense of unease through its submerged visuals, claustrophobic sound design, and unsettling atmosphere. The lighting is phenomenal, giving everything a waterlogged, dreamlike quality, while the decaying house becomes a character in its own right, soaked in grief, secrets, and decay. Rather than relying on jump scares or gore, Caveat presents its horrors in a matter-of-fact way, which somehow makes them even more disturbing. The film trusts atmosphere and ambiguity over easy explanations, and even the open-to-interpretation ending worked for me. Just a really terrific horror film. See more Read all reviews
